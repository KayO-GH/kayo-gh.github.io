My mom is a pure Ewe, from Anloga in Ghana’s Volta region. You’d think I would be able to speak the language… fat chance. I speak English, Twi, pidgin, beginner French, and a lot of programming languages (I know, I know, I don’t *speak* them, but let me tell my story, will you?). As for Ewe, I have tried and failed, multiple times. I’m still trying though. One day, in the near future (or maybe in my imagination — fingers crossed), I’ll write a story in Ewe.

Anyway, the point is that this post has nothing to do with Ewe, and all to do with Python. It just happens that the events took place in Eweland, in a town called Hohoe, at the University of Health and Allied Sciences (UHAS).

### Django Girls

There’s an international volunteer movement known as Django Girls. Django is a framework — pre-structured code and instructions — used with the Python programming language to create applications for the web. So what “Django Girls” does is to introduce women to programming using Python and the Django framework. I hope this makes sense. We’re basically teaching girls to code, and it’s pretty awesome.

My prior coaching experience was at the last Django Girls Accra event:

### The Road Trip

![Road trip](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:300/0*A7YVorZlqc4xxEjy.png)

![Road trip](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:300/0*CuXz8XQ9taFrm6XD.jpg)

![django girls](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:300/0*rVerIYXkd8F6-JaF.jpeg)

Armed with backpacks, water bottles, Google Maps, and a power bank, we set off from Accra to Hohoe in a Sprinter trotro that we boarded at Madina.

If you’re my height or taller (6-foot-3 upwards), **NEVER** agree to take a 6-hour road trip sitting in the second row of a Sprinter trotro! Never! Your legs will not forgive you. James was fine, Jacky couldn’t have been happier (she’s barely a five-footer), but me… chale, in the words of lawyer Nti Katakyie Bombay Isaiah, *“It was not a good distin”*.

Even worse was the bumpiness of the road. Look, the road was as rough as a crocodile’s back. It was so rough that one car developed a puncture in one of its tires, and the driver did not know for a full 5 minutes, because the drive was just as bumpy with or without a pumped tire. True story.

And then there were the rain storms… and at certain points clouds of dust.

But it wasn’t all bad. There were a few breath-taking views, and then there was the Adomi Bridge, under which former president J.J. Rawlings once flew a fighter jet.

### So What Exactly Did We Do? (We didn’t just take selfies)

Every coach had two or three mentees, all newbie programmers. And we took them through the basics of Python, through the Django framework, and then helped them to start building their own blogs.

![django girls](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:1400/0*iQrHcXIv5vp95e1e.jpg)

Of course, no one becomes a kickass developer in a day… that’s insane, but at least, now there are 50 more ladies with some interest in programming, who can recognize Python code when they see it. There are 50 more ladies who now have an idea of where to start if they want to begin a career in software development.

But if ever I doubted the potency of what we traveled six hours to do, I only need to look at the lady mentors to know that we did not waste our time. Save Jacky, who has been in Ghana for only two months, all the other female coaches are products of previous Django Girl boot camps. I know right?! So shout outs to the ladies who have proved that it is possible: Sefakor, Mimi, Lala, Rosie, and Abby. You guys rock! 😍

I’m not asking anyone to become a Python coach. I’m sharing this to inspire you to make an impact where you are. Brighten your “one corner”.
